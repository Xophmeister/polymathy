- Depth marker: roughly A' Level Philosophy, extended towards first-year undergraduate wherever the structure demands it — formal logic beyond the A' Level minimum, philosophy of science as a branch in its own right, philosophy of language at the level of Frege and Kripke.
- Unlike the four existing subjects, there is no prior qualification here to have atrophied. The diagnostic measured how philosophical problems are reasoned with, not what is recalled about them.

- ## The shape of this syllabus
	- Philosophy has far weaker dependency chains than the sciences. The tree is shallow: a short trunk ( ((6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02)), ((6acb85de-0a3d-4563-8e0a-98d947682be3))) and five branches that run largely in parallel — epistemology and philosophy of science ( ((6acb85de-c302-40aa-9d89-e68cb494d8fb)) – ((6acb85de-e3bf-41ec-b972-06523aee7a49))), metaphysics and mind ( ((6acb85de-31e9-40c5-a682-51c93df7de2f)) – ((6acb85de-0423-427d-8aeb-15a39c27cc92))), language ( ((6acb85de-6afa-402c-bcb5-535941e9da34))), ethics ( ((6acb85de-bbbb-4595-85a9-84b159b5a391)) – ((6acb85de-118f-4c5d-b363-e767b0713ec1))) and political philosophy ( ((6acb85de-578d-4ab7-b929-377bdecb054c)), ((6acb85de-51fd-4f35-89da-3bc95d79014c))) — plus one historical topic ( ((6acb85de-b07e-4af4-8984-e23c7add2199))) that hangs directly off the trunk.
	- The trunk matters more than its size suggests. The most frequent finding of the diagnostic was not a gap in any branch but a habit — answering a nearby question in place of the one asked — and ((6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02)) is where that is to be broken. Every later topic re-tests it.

- ## Topics
	- ### [[PHIL1.1: Arguments: validity, soundness and answering the question]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02
	  prerequisites::
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Premises and conclusion; reconstructing an argument from ordinary prose, including the premises it leaves unstated. Validity as a property of form — no possible situation with true premises and a false conclusion — and soundness as validity plus true premises, with the sharp consequence that only a sound argument obliges you to accept its conclusion. Deductive validity against inductive strength. The counterexample as the universal test: of an argument's validity, of a premise, and of a proposed principle — and the discipline of running it against your own principle before offering it. Charity in interpretation. Fallacies taught by structure rather than as a list.
		- Then the habit. Identifying exactly what claim is on the table and answering *that* — not a claim one step away from it. Why philosophers stipulate hypotheticals, understood as the controlled experiment of the subject: the stipulation isolates one variable, and fighting it reintroduces the confounders the case was built to remove.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* validity and soundness (Q1); "didn't reduce accidents" drifting to "aren't useful" and then to policy (Q1); invented facts in the performer case (Q6); widened scope in the suffering case (Q8); principles offered untested and broken by counterexamples constructible from the case itself (Q4).
	- ### [[PHIL1.2: Formal logic as a tool]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-0a3d-4563-8e0a-98d947682be3
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Propositional logic: connectives, truth tables, formalisation of English arguments, natural deduction in outline. The material conditional and exactly where it departs from English "if": its truth when the antecedent is false, and why it is the wrong reading of causal and counterfactual conditionals. Predicate logic: quantifiers, scope and its ambiguities, identity, and Leibniz's law as the logic of identity. What formalisation settles — validity — and what it cannot touch — the truth of a premise about the world.
		- The second masters (linguistics) is likely to make the scope and semantics material quicker than elsewhere; do not let that become licence to skip the formalisation.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the truth table turned on an empirical premise to decide its truth (Q1); validity, handled fluently in Q1, not brought to bear where it would have settled the question (Q8).
		- *Cross-subject:* conditional probability is not the probability of a conditional ( ((6a764603-9774-4284-916d-c03620cbb92e))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.3: From myth to argument: the pre-Socratics]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-b07e-4af4-8984-e23c7add2199
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- What changed when divine genealogy gave way to natural principle. Thales and the single underlying stuff; Anaximander's *apeiron* and the argument against any one element being primary; Heraclitus on flux and logos; Parmenides' deduction that change is impossible, taken seriously as an argument with premises to be located and denied; Zeno's paradoxes, and the arrow in particular as a problem about instantaneous motion; the atomists as the response to Parmenides. Why this counts as the beginning of philosophy and of science at once — and the case that it was less of a clean break than the story says.
		- Owned here; to be cited by `MYTH` once designed.
		- *Cross-subject:* the arrow paradox and instantaneous rate of change ( ((6a764603-68bf-4c9b-b789-01547b08ac39))); Democritus' atoms against the modern atom ( ((6a652f81-3ddd-43f8-8a38-18c1c85d37c3))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.4: Knowledge, justification and scepticism]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-c302-40aa-9d89-e68cb494d8fb
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Knowledge as justified true belief, and the Gettier cases that broke the analysis. Responses: no-false-lemmas, reliabilism, the defeasibility condition. Internalism against externalism about justification. Foundationalism and coherentism as answers to the regress. Cartesian scepticism and the brain in a vat; the closure principle as the engine of sceptical arguments, and what denying it costs. Testimony: how believing others can confer justification, and when it does not.
		- Also owned here: the distinction between what is the case and what can be known to be the case — metaphysics against epistemology — as a standing check.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* whether the original *is* the original run together with whether anyone could *tell* (Q3). Testimony was reached unprompted in Q2 and is retained; build on it.
	- ### [[PHIL1.5: Induction and its problem]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-f2e3-4948-b798-a1885864967e
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-c302-40aa-9d89-e68cb494d8fb))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Hume's problem stated so that it is felt: every inference from observed to unobserved assumes the uniformity of nature; that assumption is not a logical truth; its only support is inductive; the justification is circular. Why making the claim probabilistic does not escape — and why more careful measurement does not either. Responses: Reichenbach's pragmatic vindication; Strawson's dissolution; Bayesian updating, and what it presupposes in its priors. Goodman's new riddle and the projectibility of predicates. Inference to the best explanation as a further non-deductive form, and whether it is induction in disguise.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the whole of Q2 — the probabilistic reframing offered as an answer; inspection of the supply chain offered as an escape; the question read as about precedents of poisoning rather than about past observation as such.
		- *Cross-subject:* the sample mean as an estimator — sampling as the formalised inductive step ( ((6a764603-a8e4-4234-b650-a45bc7ce1cb8))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.6: Testing theories: falsification, underdetermination and paradigms]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-dbff-4656-87d9-67de9ad86949
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-f2e3-4948-b798-a1885864967e))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Popper's attempt to rest science on falsification rather than confirmation, as a response to ((6acb85de-f2e3-4948-b798-a1885864967e)), and the question of whether corroboration smuggles induction back in. Demarcation and why it is harder than it looks. Duhem–Quine: no hypothesis is tested alone, so a failed prediction never says which assumption failed. Kuhn: normal science, anomaly, revolution, and the incommensurability claim. Lakatos: research programmes, progressive and degenerating. Simplicity and Occam's razor — what preference for the simpler theory amounts to, and whether it is itself an inductive bet. What a statistical significance test does and does not license.
		- *Cross-subject:* what a $p$-value licenses, as against how it is computed ( ((6a764603-7297-4b85-beff-26db87c6c2f4))); Luria–Delbrück as a decisive discrimination between hypotheses ( ((6a67d966-9401-4c47-810f-89b084670196))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.7: Causation and explanation]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-06bc-46aa-a9d4-d95078d9d9b6
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-f2e3-4948-b798-a1885864967e))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Hume on causation: constant conjunction, and why necessary connection is never observed. The counterfactual analysis — $c$ caused $e$ if, had $c$ not occurred, $e$ would not have — and its problems with pre-emption and overdetermination. The counterfactual is never observed, which is the whole difficulty of causal inference: confounding, the control group as a stand-in for the unobserved world, and randomisation as the reason the stand-in is trustworthy. Regression to the mean as a case study. Correlation against causation, stated precisely. Explanation: the covering-law model, its counterexamples, and causal and unifying accounts.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* confounding was reached in Q1 from a concrete case but not stated as the principle that a causal claim is a comparison against an unobserved counterfactual; to be produced from a standing start.
		- *Cross-subject:* covariance and correlation ( ((6a764603-6a7b-471e-8b5f-e52238c76f6a))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.8: Scientific realism]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-e3bf-41ec-b972-06523aee7a49
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-dbff-4656-87d9-67de9ad86949)), ((6acb85de-06bc-46aa-a9d4-d95078d9d9b6))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Whether successful theories describe an unobservable reality or merely organise observations. The no-miracles argument for realism, and its reliance on inference to the best explanation. The pessimistic meta-induction against it: past theories were successful and false. Instrumentalism, constructive empiricism, and structural realism as the attempt to keep what survives theory change. What exactly is retained when a theory is abandoned.
		- *Cross-subject:* the luminiferous aether as the standing case for the pessimistic meta-induction ( ((6a6d2611-e5ad-436f-9a52-75e3784d847a))); instrumentalism about quantum mechanics ( ((6a6d2611-6d35-45b0-9e5a-95cfdf53cfe4))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.9: Identity and persistence]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-31e9-40c5-a682-51c93df7de2f
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-0a3d-4563-8e0a-98d947682be3))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Numerical against qualitative identity. Leibniz's law and the transitivity of identity as working tools. Persistence through change: the ship of Theseus, and the difference between sameness of matter and continuity of an object. Personal identity: the physical continuity view and the psychological continuity view, each stated with its best case. Fission and duplication, and the transitivity argument that psychological continuity cannot be sufficient for identity because it can branch and identity cannot. The no-branching clause and its oddity; Parfit's conclusion that identity is not what matters in survival.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the transitivity argument and its verdict, unreached (Q3); cell replacement offered as showing the body irrelevant when it shows only that the matter is (Q3); what the duplicate would and would not know (Q3).
	- ### [[PHIL1.10: Mind and body]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-b650-4163-8ff2-ec56e5884ecc
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-31e9-40c5-a682-51c93df7de2f))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Substance dualism and Descartes' argument for it, together with the interaction problem. Physicalism, and the identity theory as a claim about identity in exactly ((6acb85de-31e9-40c5-a682-51c93df7de2f))'s sense. Multiple realisability and functionalism. The hard problem; Mary's room and the zombie argument, each assessed as an argument with premises to deny. Mental causation and the causal closure of the physical.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* "materialism" offered as the name of a position on personal identity (Q3).
		- *Cross-subject:* conservation of energy and the causal closure argument against interactionist dualism ( ((6a6d2611-8b27-4f10-a37b-b6008c805c38))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.11: Free will and determinism]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-0423-427d-8aeb-15a39c27cc92
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-06bc-46aa-a9d4-d95078d9d9b6)), ((6acb85de-b650-4163-8ff2-ec56e5884ecc))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Determinism stated precisely, and distinguished from fatalism and from predictability. Incompatibilism: the consequence argument and the principle of alternative possibilities. Compatibilism: freedom as acting from your own reasons without coercion; Frankfurt cases as the attack on alternative possibilities. Why indeterminism does not obviously help — randomness is not control. Hard determinism and what it does to moral responsibility. The Libet experiments, and what the gap between decision and awareness does and does not show.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the whole of Q7 — determinism not identified as the real issue; a compatibilist position taken without seeing there were sides; what "freely" must mean, unanswered.
		- *Cross-subject:* radioactive decay as the standard case of genuine indeterminism ( ((6a6d2611-1992-43a0-a932-507b1ebca6c1))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.12: Meaning and reference]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-6afa-402c-bcb5-535941e9da34
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-0a3d-4563-8e0a-98d947682be3))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Frege's puzzle about informative identity statements, and the distinction between sense and reference that resolves it. Belief reports and substitution failure. Russell's theory of descriptions and the problem of empty names. Kripke: rigid designation, the causal-historical theory of names, and the arguments against descriptivism. Meaning as use, in outline.
		- The closest topic to recently used knowledge in the whole programme. Expect it to move quickly; push it accordingly.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the morning star and the evening star, unreached even with a nudge (Q5).
	- ### [[PHIL1.13: Consequentialism]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-bbbb-4595-85a9-84b159b5a391
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Right action as the action with the best consequences. Bentham and Mill; hedonism and its alternatives as theories of value. Act against rule consequentialism. Expected value as the decision rule under uncertainty. The objections stated so that they bite: demandingness, the separateness of persons, the utility monster, measurement and aggregation, and cases where maximising requires something monstrous.
		- *Cross-subject:* expected value and the linearity of expectation ( ((6a764603-6a7b-471e-8b5f-e52238c76f6a))).
	- ### [[PHIL1.14: Duties and constraints]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-d977-4d9e-b8c2-e9144def10a5
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-bbbb-4595-85a9-84b159b5a391))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Deontology as the view that some acts are forbidden whatever the consequences. Kant: the categorical imperative in its universal-law and humanity formulations; persons never to be used merely as means. Doing against allowing. The doctrine of double effect: intended means against foreseen side effect. The tram and the surgeon, and Thomson's loop case as the test of whether double effect survives. Agent-relative constraints and the paradox of deontology: why it can be wrong to violate a constraint to prevent more violations of it.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the whole of Q4 — a principle that failed to separate the cases; victim agency offered and refuted by the walking worker and the anaesthetised visitor; "sacrifice" and "murder" as labels rather than mechanism; the narrowed question about the role of the one's death, unanswered.
	- ### [[PHIL1.15: Virtue and character]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-b02a-4cb7-9b84-34d0824dd1b1
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-bbbb-4595-85a9-84b159b5a391)), ((6acb85de-d977-4d9e-b8c2-e9144def10a5))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Aristotle: virtue as a stable disposition, the doctrine of the mean, practical wisdom, and flourishing. Virtue ethics as an answer to a different question — what sort of person to be, rather than which act to perform — and the charge that it gives no guidance. The distinction between a vice of character and an injustice in action, and why an act can display the first without being the second. The situationist challenge from social psychology.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* "petulance" substituted for injustice in the performer case (Q6).
	- ### [[PHIL1.16: Metaethics: facts, values and the rationality of morality]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-118f-4c5d-b363-e767b0713ec1
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-7ee9-48c9-98bb-a0827de9fc02)), ((6acb85de-bbbb-4595-85a9-84b159b5a391)), ((6acb85de-d977-4d9e-b8c2-e9144def10a5)), ((6acb85de-b02a-4cb7-9b84-34d0824dd1b1))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Hume's is–ought gap: no set of purely descriptive premises entails a normative conclusion, and what follows if no bridging premise can be justified. Moore's open question argument. Moral realism, error theory, expressivism and relativism, each with its strongest case. The distinction between accepting a claim and endorsing an action. Whether reason requires morality: Hume's instrumental reason against Kant's practical reason, and contractarian attempts — Gauthier — to show that morality is what rational self-interest would agree to.
		- This is the topic the programme's philosophy strand started from: "there's nothing inherently rational about morality" was a metaethical thesis, offered before the argument for it was known.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the is–ought gap, unseen (Q8); accepting a factual claim run together with endorsing a policy (Q1).
	- ### [[PHIL1.17: Political authority and obligation]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-578d-4ab7-b929-377bdecb054c
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-bbbb-4595-85a9-84b159b5a391)), ((6acb85de-d977-4d9e-b8c2-e9144def10a5))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Why, if at all, the state may command and the citizen must obey. Hobbes: the state of nature, the prisoner's dilemma it amounts to, and the sovereign as the change of payoffs. Locke: consent, and the trouble with tacit consent. Fair play and associative accounts. Philosophical anarchism as the strongest case against any duty to obey. Legitimacy distinguished from justice, and from mere effectiveness; the empirical evidence on why people actually comply, and what it can and cannot settle about why they should.
		- Boundary with ((6acb85de-118f-4c5d-b363-e767b0713ec1)): whether *morality* is what rational agents would agree to is owned there; whether *political authority* rests on agreement is owned here.
	- ### [[PHIL1.18: Distributive justice]]
	  id:: 6acb85de-51fd-4f35-89da-3bc95d79014c
	  prerequisites:: ((6acb85de-578d-4ab7-b929-377bdecb054c))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- What makes a distribution of goods just. Rawls: the original position, the veil of ignorance, the difference principle, and the basic structure of society as the subject of justice. Nozick: entitlement theory, and the argument that patterned principles cannot survive free exchange without continual interference. Historical against patterned conceptions. Luck egalitarianism in outline. The replies to Nozick stated at full strength: background institutions, effects on third parties, compounding advantage.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the whole of Q6 — the clean case reached a correct intermediate judgement but not its implication for distributive justice.
