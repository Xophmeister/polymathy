- Depth marker: no school qualification maps onto this. Roughly first-year undergraduate in a classics, Old Norse or Celtic studies department for the traditions, with historical linguistics taught from the ground up to the depth the comparative method needs, and no further.
- See ((6aceb0db-e452-4b9d-badc-4dbc7541cfb8))
- ## What this subject is
	- The stories people have made to account for the world and their place in it: where those stories came from, how they travelled, and what they did for the people who told them.
	- Level 1 learns the methods on four bodies of material — Greek, Roman, Norse, and the British Isles (Irish, Welsh, Anglo-Saxon) — chosen because they are culturally familiar and because they are *related*, so that all three verdicts are live and have to be decided between. Indo-Iranian material, Vedic above all, enters as comparanda only. The ancient Near East and Christianity enter as contact sources and channels of transmission: nearly everything surviving from the north was written down by Christians, and the Greek theogony is in large part a Near Eastern import.
	- The organising question, asked of every comparison: **inherited, borrowed or independent?** And before it: what kind of evidence does the comparison rest on, and who produced it?
	- The subject's stated goal is global. Later levels widen geographically and deepen in the traditions met here (see "Deferred to Level 2").
- ## Reading
	- Most topics set a primary text in translation, sized to an evening, to be read **before** the session; sessions treat it as data. Any reputable modern translation will do. Secondary reading marked **after** is for consolidation and is not assumed in the session.
- ## Pronunciation
	- The first time a name or term appears whose pronunciation English spelling does not reliably give — in this file or in a session — the tutor follows it with an IPA transcription, once. Names with an established English form (Zeus, Odin, Demeter, Noah) are not transcribed unless the original pronunciation bears on an argument. Conventions:
		- the pronunciation of the source's own language and period: Attic Greek, Classical Latin, Vedic Sanskrit, Old Norse of c. 1200, Old and Middle Irish, Middle Welsh (approximated by modern Welsh where the two differ little), Old English;
		- pitch accent marked with tone diacritics for Greek and Vedic; stress marked with ˈ elsewhere;
		- Akkadian, Hittite and Hurrian names in the conventional scholarly reading, which is a convention rather than a reconstruction;
		- modern scholars' names in their own language;
		- reconstructed (starred) forms are not transcribed: they are formulae, not pronunciations;
		- where no defensible transcription exists, the tutor says so rather than inventing one.

- ## Topics
	- ### [[MYTH1.1: What a myth is, and what it is for]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-3180-481f-b506-e90a9c3ebbb0
	  prerequisites::
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Myth, legend and folktale distinguished, and why the distinctions are useful and leaky. Theories of myth stated as *hypotheses with predictions*, not as schools: myth as early science (Tylor, Frazer); myth as the spoken counterpart of ritual (the Cambridge ritualists); myth as social charter (Malinowski /ma.liˈnɔf.ski/); myth as structure mediating oppositions (Lévi-Strauss /le.vi stʁos/); myth as disguised history (euhemerism /juːˈhiː.mə.rɪ.zəm/ as a theory of origin — euhemerism as a Christian practice is ((6aceb0db-396a-4e7c-ad59-1316ac37ecdf))); psychological readings in outline. What evidence favours one over another for a given story, and why no single theory has to win for all stories.
		- The standing check, introduced here and applied throughout: a reading that requires discarding the part of the text the text treats as its climax is a reading in trouble. Explanations of a social fact are as rational as explanations of a natural one; the bias to watch is towards readings that make the tellers look like engineers.
		- *Before:* the Homeric *Hymn to Demeter*. *After:* Malinowski, *Myth in Primitive Psychology* (1926).
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the *Hymn to Demeter* read as a teaching aid for farmers, its ending set aside as flourish and then forced into the farming reading (Q5); a myth treated only as explanation (Q5).
		- *Cross-subject:* theories as testable hypotheses ( ((6acb85de-dbff-4656-87d9-67de9ad86949))).
	- ### [[MYTH1.2: Sources and source criticism]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-e940-4047-b56f-64d4e10bed17
	  prerequisites::
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- What survives and why: texts, inscriptions, images, archaeology, place-names, personal names. For any source, the standing questions: who produced it, when, at what distance from the material, for whom, and what they needed it to say. Checking a source for accuracy, as in the sciences, against treating it as an object of study in its own right. Dependent against independent witnesses, and the stemma as the tool for telling them apart — produced unprompted in the diagnostic, to be formalised here. *Interpretatio romana* /in.ter.preˈtaː.ti.oː roːˈmaː.na/ as a translation practice, and what a functional identification does and does not tell you. Ethnographers, chroniclers and missionaries as observers with categories of their own. Selection: who collected, what they were looking for, and what that does to an apparent distribution.
		- *Before:* Caesar, *Gallic War* 6.11–28.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* Caesar's Mercury (Q2); Adam of Bremen unquestioned (Q7); the missionary and collector clue unused (Q8); the Tuatha Dé /ˈtuə.θa dʲeː/ taken at the monks' word (Q6). Source criticism was absent four times; the learner's own hypothesis is that sources are trusted by STEM habit.
		- *Cross-subject:* testimony and when it confers justification ( ((6acb85de-c302-40aa-9d89-e68cb494d8fb))).
	- ### [[MYTH1.3: Historical linguistics: sound change and the family tree]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-793f-43f1-b493-04e153f2dcda
	  prerequisites::
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Language change as *regular*: the Neogrammarian claim that a sound change operates without exception in a given environment, and why that regularity is what makes the rest of this subject possible. Correspondence sets built from data; Grimm's law worked through, so that *pater* : *father* is derived rather than recalled. Cognate against loanword against chance resemblance. Reconstruction and the asterisk: what a starred form claims. The family tree: daughter against sister, and the commonest error in the diagnostic — drawing one sister as the parent of another. The Indo-European family, its branches, and a defensible date range for the parent. Why similarity alone proves nothing.
		- Taught from the ground up. The linguistics MA was syntax and phonology, not historical linguistics; nothing here is to be treated as known.
		- *Before:* Campbell, *Historical Linguistics: An Introduction* — the chapters on sound change and on the comparative method.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* the phonological case for *Zeus* : *Iuppiter* /ˈjʊp.pɪ.tɛr/ : *Dyauṣ* /djáwʂ/ (Q1); "Hittite → Greek" (Q4); "Welsh Celts → Irish Celts" (Q6); *god* taken as cognate with *Zeus* (Q1); Ouranos /uː.ra.nós/ and Anu /ˈa.nu/ asserted cognate without a test (Q4).
	- ### [[MYTH1.4: Morphology, borrowing and contact]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-9e47-4a76-9f75-41ee6a3bd629
	  prerequisites:: ((6aceb0db-793f-43f1-b493-04e153f2dcda))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Inflection and the paradigm. Ablaut, and irregular paradigms as archaisms: why *Zeus* : *Dios* /di.ós/ and *Nuadu* /ˈnuə.ðu/ : *Nuadat* /ˈnuə.ðəd/ are evidence of age, and why borrowings come out regular. Loanwords and their signatures: donor phonology, regularisation, the date a loan entered relative to a sound change. Calque and translation: the meaning travels, the sound does not. Contact, bilingualism and substrate.
		- Then the statement this topic exists to establish: **inheritance is vertical transmission within a lineage from a common ancestor; borrowing is horizontal transmission between communities by contact, however faithful.** Each predicts something different about similarity: inherited material differs *regularly*, borrowed material differs little, or is translated.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* inherited and borrowed held as faithful against altered copying — the central misconception of the diagnostic (Q4); Zen Buddhism classed as inherited (Q4); closeness of match read as evidence of inheritance (Q4, Q6, Q8); Ouranos and Anu as translation (Q4); textual copying called inheritance (Q3).
		- *Cross-subject:* descent with modification, and horizontal transfer as the complication to a tree ( ((6a67d966-9401-4c47-810f-89b084670196))).
	- ### [[MYTH1.5: Inherited, borrowed or independent: comparing stories]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-d3dd-43b4-8dab-abd99825e870
	  prerequisites:: ((6aceb0db-3180-481f-b506-e90a9c3ebbb0)), ((6aceb0db-e940-4047-b56f-64d4e10bed17)), ((6aceb0db-9e47-4a76-9f75-41ee6a3bd629))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- The framework moved from words to narratives, and what changes. Each verdict stated as predictions. Inheritance: cognate names, shared formulae, regular divergence, distribution following the family tree. Borrowing: a route of contact, a date, translated or replaced names, a specific sequence preserved. Independence: a *mechanism* of convergence — shared ecology, social structure or cognition — low specificity, and a distribution that ignores both family and contact. Specificity as the weight a resemblance can bear: an arbitrary sequence of events is evidence; "three things" or "a flood" is not. Elasticity of categories as the route to unfalsifiability. What a story is *about* kept apart from how the *story* travelled. Galton's problem: why parallel cases are not independent data points. The control group: looking for a pattern where the hypothesis says it should not be. Motif and tale-type indexes (Thompson; Aarne /ˈɑːr.ne/–Thompson–Uther /ˈuː.tɐ/) as tools, with their biases.
		- Worked cases: Dumézil's /dy.me.zil/ three functions (owned here, not re-litigated in ((6aceb0db-9bca-4a9e-a360-8ad495f45ef4))); the global distribution of flood stories.
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* Dumézil — elasticity reached unaided, the medieval three orders unused, borrowing confined to "adjacent trees" (Q7); "unconvincing as inheritance" taken to mean "independent" (Q7); floods without selection or contamination, and the event conflated with the story (Q8); similarity of concept or function used to discriminate (Q1, Q2, Q6); Indo-European language taken to imply an inherited pantheon (Q2).
		- *Cross-subject:* falsification and demarcation ( ((6acb85de-dbff-4656-87d9-67de9ad86949))); the control group and the unobserved comparison ( ((6acb85de-06bc-46aa-a9d4-d95078d9d9b6))).
	- ### [[MYTH1.6: Greek myth: the sources and the system]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-d751-4e90-bcac-a74de1d16e51
	  prerequisites:: ((6aceb0db-3180-481f-b506-e90a9c3ebbb0)), ((6aceb0db-e940-4047-b56f-64d4e10bed17))

	- ### [[MYTH1.7: Roman religion and Roman myth]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-38a2-4a8d-84ca-9f1512db340e
	  prerequisites:: ((6aceb0db-d3dd-43b4-8dab-abd99825e870)), ((6aceb0db-d751-4e90-bcac-a74de1d16e51))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- A religion of cult, calendar and contract rather than of narrative, and the long-running question of whether Rome had myths of its own at all. Borrowing from Greece and Etruria, and the native gods underneath: Jupiter, Mars, Quirinus /kʷɪˈriː.nʊs/, Janus, Vesta. *Interpretatio* run in the opposite direction. Myth as history: Romulus, the kings and the early Republic, and Dumézil's argument that Roman legend is Indo-European myth historicised. Ovid as a learned poet reworking Greek material, not as a witness to Roman belief.
		- *Before:* Livy, Book 1, chapters 1–21 (the founding to Numa).
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* *Mercurius* /mɛrˈkʊ.ri.ʊs/ as a Latin-internal formation (Q2); Apollo as a borrowing from Greek (Q1).
	- ### [[MYTH1.8: Norse myth: the Eddas, the skalds and Snorri]]

	- ### [[MYTH1.9: The Irish and Welsh material]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-3ffb-4a12-bd31-b5cc1abf9c16
	  prerequisites:: ((6aceb0db-e940-4047-b56f-64d4e10bed17)), ((6aceb0db-d3dd-43b4-8dab-abd99825e870))
	  state:: not started
	  note::
		- Continental and Romano-British evidence first: inscriptions, place-names, iconography, the classical ethnographers. Then the insular literatures, both written by Christians: the Irish Mythological Cycle — *Lebor Gabála Érenn* /ˈlʲe.βər ˈɡa.βaː.la ˈeː.rʲən/, *Cath Maige Tuired* /kaθ ˈma.ɣʲe ˈtu.rʲəð/ — and the Welsh Four Branches of the *Mabinogi* /ma.bɪˈnɔ.ɡi/. The Tuatha Dé Danann /ˈtuə.θa dʲeː ˈda.nən/ as gods made into a people. "Synthetic history" as the monastic project of fitting Ireland into biblical chronology. Lugus /ˈlʊ.ɡʊs/, Lug /luɣ/ and Lleu /ɬəi/; Nodens /ˈnɔ.dɛns/, Nuadu and Nudd /nɨːð/; what it means to call something the same god across Gaul, Britain and Ireland.
		- *Before:* *Cath Maige Tuired* (the Second Battle of Mag Tuired); the Fourth Branch of the *Mabinogi* (*Math fab Mathonwy* /maːθ vaːb maˈθɔ.nʊɨ/).
		- *Re-tests from diagnostic:* Nuadu, Nudd and Nodens, with the *-nt-* stem as the evidence (Q6); the Tuatha Dé as "Celtic settlers" (Q6); "Romano-British" (Q6); Lugus as Caesar's Mercury (Q2).
	- ### [[MYTH1.10: Anglo-Saxon England: myth from almost nothing]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-73aa-4599-b36f-cc1421013820

	- ### [[MYTH1.11: The ancient Near East]]

	- ### [[MYTH1.12: Christianity: tradition, transmitter, and the edge of "myth"]]

	- ### [[MYTH1.13: The sky-father and what reconstructs]]

	- ### [[MYTH1.14: Origins: kingship in heaven and the world made from a body]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-6ad5-48d8-a59b-25945353dcec
	  prerequisites:: ((6aceb0db-0bf3-4213-a731-722eba487210)), ((6aceb0db-9bca-4a9e-a360-8ad495f45ef4))
	  state:: not started

		- Two kinds of origin story with two different histories. 	- ### [[MYTH1.15: The dragon-slayer]]
	  id:: 6aceb0db-ba1f-410a-96e7-8e5bac386dfa
	  prerequisites:: ((6aceb0db-0bf3-4213-a731-722eba487210)), ((6aceb0db-9bca-4a9e-a360-8ad495f45ef4))
	  state:: not started
